What if AI didn't just assist scientists — but made the discovery itself?

Anthropic's new life sciences research group deployed ~950 Claude agents to autonomously scan a massive DNA sequence database. In just 21 hours, consuming 210 million tokens, Claude identified a previously unknown enzyme system called array-associated reverse transcriptases (ART) — found in bacteriophages and featuring CRISPR-like repeating arrays that researchers had never characterized before.

This isn't AI helping a human find a needle in a haystack. This is AI generating hypotheses, narrowing candidates from 3,500 down to 20, and flagging genuinely novel biology. MIT's Feng Zhang called it "an exciting example of how AI agents can contribute to biological discovery" and said the findings "merit further investigation."

The implications for drug discovery, synthetic biology, and scientific research at scale are profound. We're entering an era where AI doesn't just accelerate science — it does science.
