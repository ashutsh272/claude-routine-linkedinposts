What if AI could help cure diseases, map the universe, and unlock the secrets of our genome — all at once?

Anthropic just expanded its AI for Science program to 20 new partners, bringing free Claude credits to some of the world's top research institutions. Partners now include the Alfred P. Sloan Foundation, Simons Foundation, Schmidt Sciences, the US National Science Foundation, the Wellcome Sanger Institute, Francis Crick Institute, and projects mapping the human brain (FlyWire/Princeton) and Earth's climate systems (LEAP/Columbia).

What's particularly notable: this isn't just about speeding up individual researchers. Anthropic is building shared infrastructure for science — including a new Model Hardware Standard developed with HHMI Janelia that lets AI agents safely operate physical lab instruments. A discounted Team plan for scientists is also launching alongside.

Most outcomes are designed to be open — open datasets, open-source software, shared standards, and public libraries. That's a meaningful signal that AI acceleration in science should benefit everyone, not just well-funded labs.
