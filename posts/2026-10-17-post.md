What if an AI company gave outside researchers full employee-level access to audit its safety systems — and let them publish whatever they find?

That's exactly what Anthropic just did.

Six employees from METR, an independent AI safety nonprofit, now have access to Anthropic's internal hardware, office space, and tools. Their job: conduct rigorous, independent risk evaluations of frontier models. Critically, METR can publish its findings — Anthropic can only redact predefined categories like customer data or model architectures.

This matters for two reasons. First, it raises the bar for what "AI safety" actually means. Self-reported safety is not safety. Independent, expert verification is. Second, Anthropic is making the case that every frontier AI company should operate this way — not because regulators demand it, but because trustworthy AI requires it.

The model they've outlined — covering evaluator expertise, independence, access, and freedom to publish — is a practical template the broader industry can adopt.

Transparency isn't a PR move here. It's infrastructure.
