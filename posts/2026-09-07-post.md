**An AI just did something human mathematicians couldn't: produce a computer-verified proof of Fermat's Last Theorem — in 11 days.**

Announced September 4, Anthropic's Claude worked largely autonomously to generate the first end-to-end formalization of Fermat's Last Theorem in Lean, the formal proof assistant. The result: over 13 million lines of verified Lean code, 29,500 intermediate theorems — and the largest formal mathematical proof ever constructed.

This isn't a new proof of the theorem. Wiles proved it in 1995. What Claude produced is something harder to build: a machine-checkable, axiom-to-conclusion formalization that a proof assistant can verify from scratch. Dozens of parallel Claude agents consumed 6 billion output tokens over 11 days of wall-clock time.

For professionals in mathematics, software verification, and scientific research, the implications are significant: AI systems can now tackle sustained, multi-week formal reasoning tasks at a scale humans simply cannot match alone.

This is what "AI doing real knowledge work" actually looks like.
