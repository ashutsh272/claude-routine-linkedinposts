**AI just solved 10 mathematical problems that stumped humanity for decades — for $2,000.**

OpenAI dropped one of the most remarkable announcements of the year: an internal version of Astra, their next-generation model, produced machine-checkable proofs for 10 open problems in mathematics and theoretical computer science.

Each problem had resisted human progress for at least a decade. The fields covered: group theory, high-dimensional geometry, quantum complexity, lattice cryptography, extremal combinatorics, and more. The headline result? The first-ever explicit construction of a *non-sofic group* — a question introduced by Gromov in 1999 that mathematicians have pursued for over 25 years.

The proofs are publicly verifiable via machine-checkable Lean 4 certificates on GitHub (github.com/openai/ten-proofs). Total compute cost: roughly $2,000.

This isn't just a benchmark win. It signals that AI is beginning to push the genuine frontier of human knowledge in pure research — not just automating existing work, but discovering what was previously unknown.
