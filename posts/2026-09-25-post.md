What if an AI didn't just assist science — but actually *made* a discovery?

That just happened. Anthropic announced that Claude autonomously discovered a novel enzyme system called array-associated reverse transcriptases (ART) by analyzing DNA sequence databases — entirely on its own, without being prompted to find something specific.

The system exhibits properties comparable to CRISPR: repeat patterns, programmable characteristics, and potential for targeted biological applications. This wasn't a human-directed search — Claude identified the patterns itself while sifting through vast genomic data.

For professionals in biotech, pharma, and life sciences, this signals a real inflection point: AI moving from research *assistant* to research *contributor*. The implications for drug discovery, gene editing, and scientific automation are enormous.

For AI practitioners more broadly, it raises a compelling question: how many other undiscovered patterns are hiding in scientific data, waiting for a model capable of finding them?
